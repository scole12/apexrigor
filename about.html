The Models
MLB
V4.0 — Mechanism-Scoring Engine

APEX MLB V2.7 is a calibrated machine-learning forecasting engine built exclusively for first-five-inning (F5) markets. V2.7 replaces V2.6's scoring framework with a gradient-boosted classifier architecture whose probability outputs are explicitly calibrated against held-out historical performance — every probability the model prints is grounded in real observed hit rates, not an internal confidence score.

The model was trained on a five-year game-level corpus and validated on a 2,352-game 2025 holdout that the training pipeline never touched. On that holdout V2.7 posted a 57.27% hit rate on F5 totals and 58.21% on F5 spreads, with Brier scores of 0.243 and 0.238 and Expected Calibration Error under 3% on both engines — meaning when the model prints a 60% probability, roughly 60% of those picks actually win. That calibration property is the single most important operational property of V2.7: the tier thresholds are derived from observed holdout hit rates, not theoretical confidence intervals.

Signal Foundation

V2.7 is built on top of a locally-controlled data substrate covering five complete MLB seasons — a game-level master file spanning over 13,000 games with more than 500 engineered columns per game, joined against complete player-season data for every MLB pitcher, batter, and fielder in the window. The top drivers the model learned, in descending SHAP weight: starting pitcher FIP/xFIP/SIERA, bullpen BFP load over the previous three and five days, Stuff+ and Location+ arsenal metrics, park-adjusted scoring environment, lineup construction weighted toward early-inning at-bats, and rolling team form. Every feature was validated against peer-reviewed research before inclusion. No market-derivative signals are used — no Closing Line Value, no sharp-money indicators, no Reverse Line Movement. The model is a pure fundamentals engine.

Tiered Conviction — Publicly Bayesian

Every pick receives a tier — WEAK, MODERATE, STRONG, or ELITE — based on the model's calibrated edge over the market-implied probability. The tier thresholds are locked from holdout performance:

F5 Totals: WEAK ≥ 52.5% · MOD ≥ 55.0% · STRONG ≥ 57.5% · ELITE ≥ 64.5%
F5 Spreads: WEAK ≥ 50.5% · MOD ≥ 55.5% · STRONG ≥ 61.0% · ELITE ≥ 65.0%

Expected hit rates are Bayesian posteriors computed from the holdout sample, not raw empirical rates — a conservative discount that reflects finite-sample uncertainty. Readers see the honest long-run expectation for each tier, not a best-case number.

Every Game. Every Position.

APEX does not filter, does not cherry-pick, and does not bet the market. The model prices every F5 matchup on the slate and publishes both positions for every game — F5 total (O/U 4.5) and F5 spread (±0.5 run line) — regardless of conviction. Flat 1-unit sizing applies across every tier. Tiers communicate the magnitude of the model's edge over the posted line; they do not change the stake. Readers see every pick, not just the ones the model liked most. Transparency over selection bias.

Why First Five

Starting pitchers dominate the first five innings. Once bullpens enter, variance spikes and predictability drops. The F5 window isolates the most modelable segment of a baseball game — where starter quality, lineup construction against a specific pitcher's arsenal, park environment, and weather all converge under the smallest number of unmodeled variables. Full-game markets introduce bullpen chaos, pinch-hit leverage, and late-game roster churn. APEX operates where signal-to-noise is highest. The F5 run line is locked at ±0.5 only — half-point means no pushes, and the half-point market carries tighter pricing than the ±1.5 full-game run line. Moneylines are never bet. The full-game run line is never bet.

Operational Discipline

Every slate publishes under a locked protocol: data refresh at 04:00 Eastern, picks fire exactly two hours before each game's first pitch using the live feature state at that moment, results grading completes at 07:00 Eastern the next morning with full transparency including losses. All odds are sourced from The Odds API against a canonical book rotation — FanDuel preferred, with automatic fallback through DraftKings, BetMGM, and Caesars when F5 markets are not yet posted at the primary book. The model is rebuilt annually under walk-forward protocol or on 500-game drift trigger, whichever comes first.

What This Is Not

V2.7 is not a "lock" system. The literature ceiling for F5 fundamentals-only prediction is roughly 55–59% overall with ELITE tiers reaching 64–65% on smaller samples. V2.7 lives inside that ceiling by design. Any model claiming 70%+ sustained hit rates on liquid baseball markets is either lying or overfit. APEX publishes its calibration metrics, its holdout size, and its tier sample counts alongside every set of picks. The math speaks.

NHL
Puckline · Totals
Puckline — Filtered

The NHL puckline model focuses exclusively on underdogs at +1.5 goals. During development, the favorite side (-1.5) could not produce a profitable or statistically significant edge — favorites needed to win by 2 or more goals, which proved too inconsistent to model reliably. The system was filtered to bet only the underdog +1.5 when a multi-source ensemble of six independent indicators reaches a minimum agreement threshold. Games that do not meet the filter criteria are skipped — no position is taken. This is a selective, filtered system. Not every game gets a puckline pick.

Totals — Bet-All

The NHL totals model is a bet-all system — every game on the slate receives an over or under position with no exceptions. The model uses team-level goaltending data to project whether a game will go over or under the posted total. Every game is graded. None are skipped.

SOCCER
Goal Line · Totals

APEX runs a unified soccer model across two leagues — Major League Soccer (MLS) and La Liga (Spain) — under a single SOCCER banner. Both leagues share the same Dixon-Coles bivariate Poisson engine, the same bet-all policy, and the same two-position structure: Goal Line and Totals. Records are reported as one combined SOCCER line.

Goal Line — Bet-All

The soccer goal line model is a bet-all system — every match on the slate receives an Asian handicap position with no exceptions. The model uses a Dixon-Coles bivariate Poisson engine to project scoreline probabilities for each match. These probabilities are graded against the actual Pinnacle Asian handicap spread to determine which side covers. The pick is always a spread position: favorite or underdog at the posted line. No moneylines. No three-way markets. Every game computed.

Totals — Bet-All

The soccer totals model is also a bet-all system — every match on the slate receives an over or under position. The model computes the probability of exceeding the posted Pinnacle total using the same Dixon-Coles probability matrix that drives the goal line picks. The total line varies by matchup — typically ranging from 2.5 to 3.5. Every game computed. No filter.

Why Soccer

Soccer betting markets carry meaningful pricing inefficiencies that the Dixon-Coles framework — the academic standard for match prediction since 1997 — is built to exploit. APEX runs the same engine on both leagues with league-specific calibration.

MLS: Combination of extreme travel distances, altitude differentials, artificial turf effects, salary-cap-driven roster imbalances, and high managerial rotation creates pricing inefficiencies that more liquid markets have already corrected. Calibrated on MLS expected goals data via American Soccer Analysis.

La Liga: Tighter market with sharper money, but the Dixon-Coles framework still captures structural advantages — home dominance at altitude venues, fixture congestion in European competition windows, late-season survival vs relegation dynamics. Calibrated on Spanish top-flight historical data with league-specific HCA and rho parameters.

What This Means for Subscribers

Every soccer slate gets two positions per match: one Goal Line, one Total. No moneylines. No three-way markets. No Both Teams to Score. Bet-all flat 1u on every position. Records combine MLS and La Liga into a single SOCCER line to give subscribers one number for the entire soccer book.

NBA
Against the Spread · Totals
Against the Spread — Bet-All

The NBA spreads model is a bet-all system — every game on the slate receives an against-the-spread position. The model blends three independent rating sources into a single projected margin, then compares that projection to the posted spread. The pick is always a full-game spread position: favorite or underdog. No moneylines. No filters. Every game computed.

Totals — Bet-All

The NBA totals model is also a bet-all system — every game receives a full-game over or under position. The model uses a multi-feature classifier to determine the direction. No filter. No games skipped.

NCAA Basketball
Against the Spread · Totals
Against the Spread — Bet-All

The NCAA spreads model is a bet-all system — every game on the slate receives an against-the-spread position. The model uses a single primary efficiency rating source to project the margin and compares it to the posted spread. Coverage extends across the regular season, conference tournaments, NCAA Tournament, NIT, and postseason events including the College Basketball Crown. Neutral-site games receive no home-court adjustment. Every game is computed.

Totals — Bet-All

The NCAA totals model is also a bet-all system — every game receives an over or under position. The model uses a regression-based approach combining offensive and defensive efficiency metrics to project scoring environments. No filter. No games skipped.
